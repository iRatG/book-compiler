# Domain Modeling Made Functional — Central Questions

## Question 1: How Do You Really Understand the Domain?
**Why it matters:** Misunderstanding domain requirements creates systems that don't match business reality.

---

## Question 2: How Do You Keep Domain Knowledge in Code?
**Why it matters:** When domain experts leave, code must carry their knowledge.

---

## Question 3: What's the Difference Between Problem Space and Solution Space?
**Why it matters:** Confusing them leads to technical solutions to business problems.

---

## Question 4: How Should You Model Business Workflows?
**Why it matters:** Code structure should match how business actually works.

---

## Question 5: When Should You Use Events vs. State?
**Why it matters:** Events reveal causality; state hides it.

---

## Question 6: How Do You Prevent Invalid States?
**Why it matters:** If invalid state is possible, it will eventually occur.

---

## Question 7: When Should Type Systems Enforce Business Rules?
**Why it matters:** Compiler-enforced rules prevent categories of bugs.

---

## Question 8: How Do You Keep Domain Logic Pure?
**Why it matters:** Pure logic is testable, reusable, and reliable.

---

## Question 9: How Do You Organize Bounded Contexts?
**Why it matters:** Boundaries prevent one context's changes from affecting others.

---

## Question 10: When Should You Split or Merge Contexts?
**Why it matters:** Wrong boundaries create unnecessary coupling.

---

## Question 11: How Do You Design Commands and Events?
**Why it matters:** Commands and events are how domain logic communicates.

---

## Question 12: How Do You Handle Domain Workflows?
**Why it matters:** Workflows are how value flows through the system.

---

## Question 13: When Is Refactoring the Domain Model Worth It?
**Why it matters:** Domain understanding evolves; model must evolve with it.

---

## Question 14: How Do You Validate Domain Rules?
**Why it matters:** Validation is where business rules meet reality.

---

## Question 15: How Do You Test Domain Logic?
**Why it matters:** Domain logic is most critical; must be thoroughly tested.
