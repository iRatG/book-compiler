## Supporting Arguments

**Statement:** Gogol constructs an implicit logical argument: If a stranger appears financially secure and competent in bearing, then the innkeeper will treat him with automatic civility and accommodating service. Chichikov exhibits these markers; therefore, he receives immediate deference. The argument presupposes that civility is conditional on financial status, not on moral worth.

Unremarkableness combined with calculated reserve creates power.

#### Evidence: The Innkeeper's Calculated Civility

**Statement:** "A gentleman of a certain age... made his appearance in the carriage. The inn-keeper, a stout man with a face the colour of roasted coffee, came out to meet his guest with that peculiar civility which is the reward of innkeepers who have been well paid."

This evidence directly demonstrates that civility is transactional and conditional on the perception of wealth. The innkeeper's behavior is not motivated by Chichikov's character but by his appearance of financial security.

#### Evidence: Chichikov's Calculated Questions About Serfs

**Statement:** "As Chichikov sat in the parlour of the inn, smoking a cigar and looking out at the streets of the town, he seemed to be observing everything with the eye of a man who was calculating its value and importance... he asked many questions about the affairs of the town, about the principal landowners in the neighbourhood, about their characters, their circumstances, and particularly about the number of souls—that is to say, the number of male serfs—that each of them possessed."

This evidence reveals Chichikov's calculating mindset and his specific economic interest in serfs as countable units. His questions about "souls" are presented as notable and suspicious precisely because they reduce human beings to numbers.

## Summary: How Gogol Constructs His Argument

Gogol's reasoning in this chapter operates through four levels:

1. **Logical Arguments** that show how deference follows from assumed wealth and how boredom leads to speculation

2. **Concrete Evidence** that demonstrates the town's behavior, the innkeeper's calculations, and Chichikov's character through direct observation and narration

3. **Illustrative Examples** that make abstract principles tangible (the servants' bewilderment, the town's turnaround, the innkeeper's precision)

4. **Unstated Assumptions** that reveal the chapter's foundational premises: that society is structured by economic calculation, that mystery is more powerful than truth, that isolation breeds psychological hunger for novelty

Together, these elements construct a **satirical argument** about provincial Russian society: It is a system driven by economic self-interest, entertainment-starvation, and the deference to wealth regardless of moral character. Into this system, Gogol introduces Chichikov—simultaneously ordinary and mysterious—who will exploit its fundamental vulnerabilities.
