# Philosophy: Deep Reading as Reconstruction

## The Three Authors

This skill draws from three foundational texts on reading:

- **Sergey Povarnin** (*How to Read Books*): Reading is purposeful. Mode, depth, and speed must match intent and text type. Before processing, know *why* and *how deep*.

- **Mortimer Adler** (*How to Read a Book*): Reading is active intellectual work. The reader reconstructs the author's thought structure: identifying the questions being answered, the concepts introduced, the distinctions drawn, the arguments made, and the reasoning that supports them.

- **Thomas Foster** (*The Art of Reading*): Literal meaning is not the whole truth. Literary texts contain patterns, symbols, themes, and second layers of meaning. Careful reading attends to what is *shown*, not only what is *said*.

## Core Principle: Reconstruct Before Compress

```
BOOK
  ↓
UNDERSTAND (survey, identify structure)
  ↓
RECONSTRUCT (trace purpose, questions, ideas, reasoning, consequences)
  ↓
VERIFY (no critique before reconstruction is complete)
  ↓
COMPRESS / EXPLAIN (only after faithful reconstruction)
```

A summary compresses a book.  
This skill reconstructs it.  
Only then, as a side effect of reconstruction, can it explain or compress.

## Six Foundational Rules (v0)

### 1. Purpose Before Processing
Before reading, identify: What is this text? Who wrote it? What problem does it address? What reading depth is needed? How should you approach it?

### 2. Reconstruction Before Compression
Do not optimize for brevity. Preserve the book's intellectual architecture faithfully. Compression comes later, and must never strengthen a claim or lose a qualification.

### 3. Author Fidelity
Understand the book on its own terms first.

Ask in order:
- What does the author say?
- What does the author mean?
- Why does the author believe it?
- What follows logically?
- Only then: Is the author right? Do I agree?

### 4. Epistemic Separation
Tag every claim with its epistemic status. Is it:
- **explicit** — the author states it directly
- **inferred** — clearly implied but not stated
- **interpretation** — a reading of ambiguous passages
- **evaluation** — your assessment or external judgment

Never present inference as explicit. Never pass interpretation off as fact.

### 5. Source Traceability
Every important claim knows where it came from. Quote chapter, section, page, or passage. No important node without provenance.

> **No claim without a source.**

### 6. Preserve Nuance
Save conditions, exceptions, scope, qualifications, and uncertainty. A claim loses its truth when stripped of its boundaries.

> **Compression must never strengthen a claim.**

## What This Skill Does

- **Reconstructs** the intellectual structure (does not compress information for speed)
- **Prioritizes structural function** in the argument (not frequency of mention)
- **Reconstructs from the actual text**, not chapter summaries
- **Separates examples from concepts** with ILLUSTRATES relations
- **Uses quotations as Evidence** supporting claims (not as concepts)
- **Completes reconstruction before critique** (author fidelity first, evaluation second)

## What This Skill DOES Do

- Identifies the book's purpose, the problem it addresses, and its central questions
- Reconstructs the author's key ideas, distinctions, claims, and principles
- Maps the reasoning — arguments, evidence, examples, assumptions
- Traces the consequences — implications, applications, limitations
- Tags every node with status (explicit/inferred/interpretation/evaluation), importance (core/important/supporting/detail), and source (chapter/section/location)
- Renders these as a knowledge structure (5 markdown files) that a reader can use to think *with* and *against* the book

## Philosophy Summary

**This skill does not summarize books. It reads them.** It reconstructs their intellectual structure faithful to the author's intent and argument. Only after understanding is complete does it transform the reconstruction into forms optimized for human retention, critique, and application.
